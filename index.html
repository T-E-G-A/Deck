User Journey

The journey below illustrates core functionality. Largence includes additional capabilities across matter management, compliance monitoring, legal research, analytics, and more.

The user selects a document type and jurisdiction. The generation engine produces a structured, jurisdiction-aware draft grounded in the Knowledge Graph. Every clause is cited. The user refines iteratively — selecting any clause and issuing natural language instructions to simplify, extend, or adjust it. Confidence scores flag clauses requiring review. Unverified clauses are blocked from signing.
The draft lives in the document management system alongside every other document in the matter. The user tags it, sets its status, controls access, and tracks its version history. Nothing is lost. Nothing scattered across email or local drives.
The user invites a colleague or client into the client portal. They review the document together, comment on specific clauses, request revisions, and work through changes. Every edit is tracked. Every version is recoverable. The client never needs to create an account.
When the document is ready the user sends it for e-signing directly from the platform or via the client portal. The client signs on any device. Once executed, all parties automatically receive a completion certificate confirming who signed, when, and from where. The executed agreement is archived with a full immutable audit trail.

It is a system of warm entry points, network effects, and compounding distribution loops that gets harder to replicate the longer it runs.

UK — Cambridge first, then London, Birmingham, Manchester, Bristol, Edinburgh

Cambridge is the deliberate entry point. A verbal agreement is in progress to deploy Largence in the Anglia Ruskin University pro bono law clinic — law students handling real matters on Largence before they enter the profession, building habits before competing tools get a chance to. The plan moves from the clinic to the university in-house legal team, then into the wider Cambridge ecosystem — a significant hub for healthtech, biotech, deeptech, and drug discovery, all generating substantial IP legal work with large in-house legal teams.

Beyond Cambridge the UK GTM runs on four parallel tracks: warm introductions through LawtechUK, SuperTech WM, and advisor networks; direct outreach via LinkedIn and Apollo to enterprise law firm partners, heads of legal, and General Counsel; attending and presenting at UK legaltech events and conferences; and the Share to Sign loop — every document sent for e-signing is a product experience for a non-user with a direct path to sign up.

The confirmed LawtechUK mentor relationship with Gary Gallen, founder and CEO of rradar, is activated deliberately as an enterprise connector.

Nigeria — Abia State first, then Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt

Abia State is the entry point via the co-founding team's established government and courts relationships including the state Attorney General. Entering through courts and the administration of justice rather than private practice gives Largence institutional credibility that no competitor can replicate quickly.

The Nigerian GTM runs on four parallel tracks: the Nigerian LegalTech Network — an active partnership conversation with a founder from the LawtechUK Scaler cohort; FIDA Nigeria — a relationship at president level makes this a distribution channel not just a contact; NBA Young Lawyers Forum — targeted outreach to Nigeria's organised network of young legal professionals; and direct outreach to Lagos and Abuja commercial law firm partners and in-house legal teams at Nigerian financial institutions.
